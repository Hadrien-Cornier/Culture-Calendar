    THE GOOD, THE BAD AND THE UGLY

    AFS · Film

    • Tue, Nov 3 · 6:30 PM
    • Fri, Nov 6 · 8:30 PM
    • Sun, Nov 8 · 1:30 PM

    Leone's epic western where greed, honor, and violence meet in a dusty cemetery.

    🎭 Artistic Merit. Leone turns duration into a weapon. The opening takes its time introducing Tuco, Blondie, and Angel Eyes as comic predators, then expands their pursuit across deserts, prisons, battlefields, and a ruined cemetery. Tonino Delli Colli’s widescreen images alternate between vast landscapes and extreme closeups, making geography and facial expression equally oppressive. The bridge sequence, the Union army’s meaningless slaughter, and the cemetery duel show a director who treats spectacle as choreography rather than decoration. Ennio Morricone’s score supplies the film’s nervous system, especially in the cemetery, where editing, glances, hands, and musical rhythm converge. The episodic middle section is overextended, but the control of scale, sound, and suspense remains extraordinary.

    ✨ Originality. The film dismantles the clean moral architecture of the classical Western. Blondie is calculating, Tuco is grotesque and vulnerable, Angel Eyes is efficient rather than mythic, and none of them fits a stable heroic category. Leone combines slapstick cruelty, Civil War carnage, operatic suspense, and grotesque closeup photography without smoothing their contradictions. The final three way duel, with its circular staging and escalating insert shots, converts a familiar gunfight into a ritual of calculation. Its innovations became defining conventions of the revisionist Western, even as later imitators often copied only the poses and music.

    📚 Cultural Significance. The film helped establish the international stature of the Italian Western and permanently altered the genre’s visual vocabulary. Its stylized violence, moral ambiguity, and satire of Western mythology challenged the polished heroics of Hollywood tradition. Its influence extends through filmmakers including Martin Scorsese, Robert Zemeckis, Sam Raimi, and Robert Rodriguez. The movie’s reach is also unusually durable, with its images, character types, music, and cemetery showdown repeatedly cited across popular culture. Its status is earned less by historical realism than by the force with which it made artificiality into a style.

    💡 Intellectual Depth. The film’s ideas emerge through action rather than speeches. The Civil War sections expose official violence as absurd mass slaughter, while the gold hunt reduces loyalty, nationalism, and friendship to temporary bargains. The title’s moral labels are deliberately unstable, since every protagonist behaves according to appetite, calculation, or survival. Tuco’s comic suffering complicates the film’s cynicism, and Blondie’s occasional mercy prevents the narrative from collapsing into pure nihilism. The weakness is that its characters remain primarily theatrical figures, not psychologically developed subjects. Yet the film’s central argument is clear and durable, the frontier is not a realm of moral order, but a marketplace where violence, performance, and chance determine value.

    FAUST with a live score by The Invincible Czars

    AFS · Film

    • Wed, Nov 4 · 8:00 PM

    Murnau's Faust, live-scored by The Invincible Czars, makes studio craft cosmic.

    🎭 Artistic Merit Murnau’s Faust turns studio construction into a system of visual argument. Mephisto’s wings spread over the plague stricken village, smoke rolls across miniature rooftops, and Faust’s laboratory shifts between cramped human space and cosmic theater. The film’s chiaroscuro, layered compositions, model work, matte effects, and controlled camera movement give abstract forces physical weight. The Invincible Czars’ live score adds a volatile modern counterpoint, combining classical instruments, electric guitar, drums, bossa nova, and metal without merely imitating silent film accompaniment. The film’s weakness is structural. Its early apocalypse and later Gretchen melodrama do not always belong to the same dramatic scale, and the romance sometimes reduces moral conflict to sentimental redemption.

    ✨ Originality The film’s imagery remains startling because it treats special effects as theology rather than decoration. Mephisto becomes a landscape, plague becomes smoke, and temptation becomes a change in visual space. Murnau’s elaborate sets and repeated studio experimentation expanded what cinematic illusion could accomplish, influencing later effects practice. The film does not avoid cliché at the level of plot. It inherits the pact with the Devil, the fallen woman, the innocent child, and the redemptive kiss. Its originality lies in how aggressively it reorganizes those familiar elements through scale, lighting, miniatures, and visual metamorphosis.

    📚 Cultural Significance Faust stands as a major achievement of German Expressionist cinema and a key example of silent film using cinematic form to compete with literature, theater, and painting. Its effects work and studio methods affected subsequent filmmaking, while its image of Mephisto towering over a town remains a durable model for representing evil as an environmental force. The live performance by The Invincible Czars also demonstrates the film’s unusual adaptability. Their score does not preserve the work as a museum piece, it reopens its visual rhythm for a contemporary audience.

    💡 Intellectual Depth The film’s central conflict is not simply good versus evil. Faust seeks knowledge, protection, youth, sexual pleasure, and relief from responsibility, then discovers that each gain enlarges the damage around him. The plague makes private ambition public, while Gretchen’s tragedy exposes the cost of treating another person as evidence of one’s own redemption. Murnau’s final emphasis on love offers moral closure, but it also narrows the film’s most interesting questions about freedom, guilt, and self deception. Even so, the collision between Christian cosmology, erotic appetite, scientific desire, and theatrical spectacle gives the film greater complexity than its melodramatic plot initially suggests.

    CENTER STAGE

    AFS · Film

    • Thu, Nov 5 · 9:00 PM
    • Sat, Nov 7 · 1:30 PM

    Maggie Cheung channels a silent star through fractured film and rehearsal.

    🎭 Artistic Merit Stanley Kwan builds the film from competing textures, reconstructed scenes, surviving silent footage, interviews, rehearsals, and direct address. The switches between Ruan Lingyu’s 1930s performances and Maggie Cheung’s modern preparation prevent the biopic from settling into polished historical pageantry. Cheung’s control is exceptional, especially in the scenes drawn from The Goddess, where her restrained gestures and lowered gaze turn imitation into interpretation. The 154 minute length occasionally loosens the dramatic pressure, but the formal design remains precise and demanding.

    ✨ Originality The film rejects the standard rise and fall biography. Kwan repeatedly exposes the machinery of remembrance, showing actors, researchers, and the director constructing Ruan while questioning how much of her can still be recovered. His own presence complicates the authority of the account, and Cheung’s dual position as actress and investigator makes the film’s central performance unstable in the most productive way. The documentary fiction hybrid feels structurally necessary, not decorative.

    📚 Cultural Significance Center Stage restores Ruan to more than the status of a tragic celebrity. It connects her career to Shanghai’s silent cinema, the politics of representation, the tabloid culture that pursued her, and Hong Kong cinema’s own anxiety about inheritance. By placing Ruan’s surviving images beside Cheung’s reenactments, Kwan makes film history a living argument between generations. Cheung’s performance also became a major landmark in Hong Kong acting, winning her the Berlin Best Actress award, the first such honor awarded to a Chinese woman.

    💡 Intellectual Depth The film asks whether an actress can be separated from the images, gossip, and male narratives that consume her. Ruan’s suicide is not treated as a tidy tragic climax. Instead, Kwan presents it as the result of pressures reproduced by studios, newspapers, intimate relationships, and later filmmakers. The film’s deepest insight lies in its refusal to claim final access to Ruan. Every reconstruction reveals both her presence and the distance separating the present from her life. That uncertainty gives the film its intellectual force.

    CARRIE

    AFS · Film

    • Mon, Nov 2 · 7:00 PM

    De Palma's prom bloodbath turns adolescent humiliation into a ruinous spectacle.

    🎭 Artistic Merit, De Palma gives the material real formal punch, especially in the prom sequence, where the slow buildup, split focus, and sudden blood dump turn adolescent humiliation into a coordinated crash of image and sound. The final stretch, Carrie’s return home, her mother’s knife attack, and the house collapsing into fire and debris, shows a director who knows how to escalate a scene past shock into ruin. Piper Laurie’s performance adds hard-edged theatrical force, while Sissy Spacek keeps Carrie’s shy, watchful fragility believable enough to make the violence land.

    ✨ Originality, The film avoids generic teen-horror routine by binding telekinesis to embarrassment, religion, and sexual panic, then making the prom feel less like a set piece than a social execution. The ending, with Sue’s nightmare of Carrie’s hand bursting from the grave, became one of horror’s most copied jolts because it is simple, brutal, and perfectly timed.

    📚 Cultural Significance, Carrie became a landmark horror title and one of the defining films of 1976, widely praised on release and still treated as a key Stephen King adaptation. Its influence is visible in later school-horror, revenge, and prom-night nightmare stories, and its image of the blood-soaked girl at the center of a crowd has become part of the genre’s visual vocabulary.

    💡 Intellectual Depth, The film’s ideas are plain but not thin, it studies cruelty as a group act, shame as a trigger, and faith as a weapon when filtered through Carrie’s mother. What gives it weight is the clash between private humiliation and public spectacle, a social mechanism made literal when the prank at the prom turns a teenage outcast into an agent of destruction.

    NYRB Book Club - G.

    AlienatedMajesty · Books

    • Sat, Nov 7 · 7:00 PM

    Berger’s picaresque studies desire against the Milan workers’ revolt and Garibaldi’s legacy.

    G. is a sharp, restless novel that turns a seduction plot into a study of appetite, politics, and historical change. Berger gives the book real force by setting G.’s erotic career against the Milan workers’ revolt of 1898, Garibaldi’s legacy, the Boer War, and the first flight across the Alps, so private desire keeps brushing against public history.

    🎭 Artistic Merit, 8/10. Berger’s prose, at least in the book’s own description and critical summaries, aims for compression and range, moving from sex scenes to political upheaval without losing control of the thread. The structure leans picaresque, and that suits the material, because G. is less a rounded psychological portrait than a figure carried through encounters, arguments, and historical crosscurrents.

    ✨ Originality, 8/10. The novel takes the Don Juan figure and strips him of glamour, making him a modern, almost schematic wanderer whose appeal lies in motion, not romance. That approach gives the book a clean conceptual edge, even when the form is closer to literary experiment than to traditional realism.

    📚 Cultural Significance, 8/10. G. won the 1972 Booker Prize, which places it squarely in the postwar English novel canon. Berger’s standing as a novelist and critic also matters here, because the book sits at the junction of fiction, political thought, and art criticism.

    💡 Intellectual Depth, 8/10. The book is interested in how intimacy works inside history, not outside it, and that gives its sexual episodes a social charge. Its best claim is not novelty of subject, but the way it makes pleasure, loneliness, and class movement part of the same argument.

    G. is strong, and at its best more searching than polished. It can feel schematic in its title figure, but the intelligence of its design, and the way it ties appetite to epoch, earns it a high mark.
